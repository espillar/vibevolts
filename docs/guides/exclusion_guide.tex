\section{Introduction}
This document details the geometric, vector, and angular calculations used in VibeVolts to
determine whether a target point in space is visible to a satellite or ground-based observatory
detector. Visibility is subject to two conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Field-of-View (FOV) Check}: The target must lie within the sensor's cone.
    \item \textbf{Exclusion Checks}: The line of sight must clear celestial exclusion zones
    (Sun, Moon, Earth limb) and local horizon limits for ground observatories.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Step 1: Coordinate Frame and Vectors}
Let $\mathbf{x}_{\text{obs}}$ be the observer position (satellite or observatory) and
$\mathbf{x}_{\text{targ}}$ be the target position, both in GCRS. The vector to target is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}_{\text{targ}} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{targ}} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{obs}}
    \quad [\text{meters}]
\end{equation}
and the unit pointing vector is
$\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{targ}} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{targ}} / \|\mathbf{v}_{\text{targ}}\|$.
If $\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{targ}}\| < 10^{-9}$ m, the target is co-located and marked excluded.

\section{Step 2: Field-of-View (FOV) Cone Check}
Let $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ be the unit pointing vector of the detector and $\text{FOV}$ be its
full-width angular diameter in radians. A target is within the FOV if and only if:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{targ}} = \arccos(\hat{\mathbf{p}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{targ}})
    < \frac{\text{FOV}}{2}
\end{equation}

\section{Step 3: Apparent Angular Radii of Celestial Bodies}
For a celestial body at GCRS position $\mathbf{x}_{\text{body}}$ with radius $R_{\text{body}}$,
the vector from the observer is
$\mathbf{v}_{\text{body}} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{body}} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{obs}}$.
In \texttt{exclusion.py}, \texttt{compute\_exclusions()} computes the apparent angular radius
$\theta_{\text{body}}$ using either the small-angle tangent form (used by \texttt{exclusion()}
and \texttt{update\_exclusion\_table()}) or exact spherical limb geometry when
\texttt{use\_arcsin=True} (used by \texttt{scandetectors()} for Earth-limb occlusion):
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{body}} =
    \begin{cases}
        \arcsin\!\left(\min\!\left(\dfrac{R_{\text{body}}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{body}}\|},
        1\right)\right), & \text{if } \texttt{use\_arcsin=True}, \\[1em]
        \arctan\!\left(\dfrac{R_{\text{body}}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{body}}\|}\right),
        & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Physical radii used are $R_{\text{Sun}} = 0$ (point source),
$R_{\text{Moon}} \approx 1{,}737{,}400\text{ m}$, and
$R_{\text{Earth}} \approx 6{,}378{,}137\text{ m}$ centered at $[0,0,0]$.

\section{Step 4: Exclusion Angle Check}
Let $\psi_{\text{body}} = \arccos(\hat{\mathbf{p}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{\text{body}})$ be
the angular separation between the line of sight and the body center, and $\theta_{\text{limit}}$
be the detector exclusion angle (\texttt{solarEx}, \texttt{lunarEx}, \texttt{earthEx} in radians).
For space-based detectors (and for Sun/Moon checks generally), the direction is excluded if:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_{\text{body}} - \theta_{\text{body}} < \theta_{\text{limit}}
\end{equation}

\section{Step 5: Ground Observatory Horizon Check \& Unified Packing}
For ground-based observatories (\texttt{is\_ground = True}), \texttt{compute\_exclusions()}
replaces the final body check (Earth limb) with a local-horizon elevation check. Using the
local zenith unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{obs}} / \|\mathbf{x}_{\text{obs}}\|$
and minimum elevation $\theta_{\text{min}} = \texttt{earthEx}$, a direction $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ is
excluded when its zenith angle exceeds $\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta_{\text{min}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \arccos(\hat{\mathbf{p}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{z}}) > \frac{\pi}{2} - \theta_{\text{min}}
\end{equation}

To eliminate duplicated state unpacking across callers, \texttt{check\_sim\_exclusions()}
in \texttt{exclusion.py} packs \texttt{obs\_pos}, \texttt{is\_ground}, \texttt{exclusion\_angles},
and celestial \texttt{body\_positions}/\texttt{body\_radii} once from \texttt{sim\_data} and
delegates to \texttt{compute\_exclusions()} for \texttt{exclusion()},
\texttt{update\_exclusion\_table()}, and \texttt{scandetectors()} (with \texttt{earth\_only=True}).
